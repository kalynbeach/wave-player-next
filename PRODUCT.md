# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is Kalyn, a musician and software developer on macOS who listens back to original music, Ableton exports, masters, mixes, stems, and experiments. The core workflow is focused listening and visual performance with personal files that remain in place on the local device. Open-source contributors are a future secondary audience, but the first release is allowed to be opinionated around Kalyn's workflow.

## Product Purpose

Wave Player Next is a dependable local-first audiovisual music system. It indexes an approved folder without mutating its contents, makes personal recordings easy to browse and play, and turns the active signal into a controllable visual instrument. The first release succeeds when it becomes useful for everyday listening through one persistent library, trustworthy browser-native transport, and one expressive oscilloscope/Lissajous scene.

## Positioning

Wave Player Next treats personal recordings as a durable music library and a live visual instrument, without requiring a streaming service, cloud account, or custom audio transport.

## Brand Personality

Precise, authored, and instrument-like. The product should feel calm during ordinary listening, responsive under direct manipulation, and expressive inside the visualization surface. Its voice is concise and technically honest: loading, unsupported formats, unavailable files, and renderer limitations are stated plainly.

## Anti-references

- A generic streaming-service clone organized around discovery feeds, subscriptions, or remote catalogs.
- A conventional dashboard with a permanent sidebar, inspector, or grid of unrelated cards around the player.
- Literal collectible-card styling, including fantasy ornament, foil effects, rarity badges, game mechanics, or imitation of Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, or Magic: The Gathering.
- Decorative motion that delays frequent transport or keyboard actions.
- A visualizer treated as a passive screensaver or decorative placeholder.
- A dense audio-engine laboratory that exposes transport implementation details to ordinary listening workflows.

## Design Principles

1. Reliable listening comes before specialized transport technology.
2. One bounded player card preserves track identity and transport while its working view changes.
3. The visualization is an interactive instrument with meaningful controls and saved state.
4. Product state is explicit: loading, empty, unavailable, unsupported, and error conditions stay understandable and actionable.
5. Familiar controls and quiet surrounding surfaces let the selected music and live signal carry the identity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The complete player remains usable without seeing the visualizer. All transport, view, library, and scene controls need unambiguous accessible names, visible focus, keyboard operation, and states that do not rely on color alone. Seek and volume controls expose useful ranges and values. Reduced-motion preferences remove spatial movement without obscuring state changes, and the responsive card preserves readable text and practical touch targets at narrow widths.
